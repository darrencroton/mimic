# sage26 Prescription Classification

**Status:** Complete. Extends Open Question 1 of [`MIMIC-COUPLED-RATE-FORMULATION-PLAN.md`](MIMIC-COUPLED-RATE-FORMULATION-PLAN.md) from `sage16` to `sage26`, the input step 7 needs for a successor package.
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Scope:** Every prescription in the shipped `sage26` package, classified as **rate**, **jump**, **algebraic** or **forcing** by its state-reset semantics. Analysis only — no code changes, and none are implied. The coupled-rate work remains gated on its own measurement spike.

---

## Why this document exists

[`SAGE16-PRESCRIPTION-CLASSIFICATION.md`](SAGE16-PRESCRIPTION-CLASSIFICATION.md) settled Open Question 1 for `sage16`. The plan's *First Work* section warns that a small residual measured for `sage16` "says nothing about a stiffer successor"; `sage26` is that successor, and it adds prescriptions of kinds `sage16` does not have: random labels that steer which reservoir a flux uses, a per-substep history buffer, redshift-scaled feedback and a second hot-gas reservoir. This document applies the same criterion, the same four categories and the same standard of evidence to it, and records only the cross-cutting findings the `sage16` document does not already make.

The criterion is unchanged: a **jump** resets state discontinuously and is not reachable by shrinking the step; a **rate** scales with the interval and has a limit as `dt → 0`; a threshold alone makes neither. See the `sage16` document, *The criterion, and the trap it avoids*, for the categories table.

## Eight cross-cutting findings

The `sage16` document's five findings carry over: the operator-split fluxes are named (the package declares ten transport fields, `model_properties.yaml:35`, `:47`, `:130`, `:257`, `:269`, `:309`, `:321`, `:332`, `:455`, `:467`), the `min`/clamp sites remain, `Rheat` and the slaved feedback are still non-pure, the FoF group is still the integration domain, and the jumps remain few. What follows is new.

### 1. Two random labels, redrawn every snapshot, steer the rates

`Regime` (#5) and `FFBRegime` (#7) are drawn afresh each snapshot from deviates keyed on the snapshot number (`shared/sage_keyed_draw.h:97-102`; `shared/sage_ffb_regime.h:216`), with no hysteresis. Within an interval each is constant; between intervals a galaxy near `M_shock` or `M_ffb` flips with probability up to one half per snapshot. They move no mass themselves, but `Regime` picks the source or destination of #8, #9, #10, #14, #15, #18, #20, #21, #22 and #23 (`shared/sage_cgm_routing.h:105-107`), and a flip of the central's label fires the wholesale hot↔CGM transfer of #6 (`sage26_prepare_infall_budget.c:165-177`); `FFBRegime` swaps one whole star-formation chain for another (#15).

Under the target formulation both are **forcing**, `h(t)`, applied at snapshot boundaries. Two constraints follow. The classifier's inputs and the labels it sets must stay fixed over the interval: the deviates are pure functions of their keys (`shared/sage_keyed_draw.h`, `shared/sage_ffb_regime.h`), so re-evaluating them with the same keys returns the same value, but re-evaluating the classifier with interval-updated masses would move the boundary comparison and relabel mid-interval. And because the flip rate is per snapshot, the realised regime history depends on the tree's snapshot spacing, which is a property of the simulation rather than of the physics; a continuous formulation can keep that only by keeping the labels at snapshot boundaries. Only the central's gas is consolidated at a flip (`sage26_prepare_infall_budget.c:158-177`), so a satellite's label and the reservoir that holds its gas can disagree (`sage26_satellite_stripping.c:122-126`).

### 2. State that is not a reservoir, which the integrator must carry

`sage26` keeps galaxy memory that is neither mass in a reservoir nor a function of current state: the 60 SFR-history floats (`SfrDiskBin0` onward, `model_properties.yaml:708`), sampled every substep (`shared/sage_sfr_history.h:168-170`, `:197-199`), reset only for rows that join as Type 0 or 1 (`sage26_initialise_merger_clock.c:152-154`), so an orphan keeps its bins across snapshots (`shared/sage_sfr_history.h:212-215`), and moved by mergers (`shared/sage_merger_physics.h:375-385`); the ICS mass×time moment (`:489-490`); `PreviousType` and the infall latches (#1, #26); the mass-weighted instability-bulge radius (`shared/sage_bulge_size_physics.h:363`, `:375`); and the substep-summed `Cooling`, `Heating`, `SupernovaOutflowRate` and `StarFormationRate` (`sage26_apply_star_formation_supernova.c:255-256`). None of it belongs in a conservation ledger, and every item is an integral or a point sample of the evolution, or a value latched at an event. A declared-transfer package must therefore declare **observer state** — quadratures of declared fluxes and values latched by jump maps — alongside reservoirs, or lose these outputs. This bears directly on Open Question 2.

### 3. The SFR-history binning depends on the substep count

A substep's bin is `⌊step·10/N⌋` (`shared/sage_sfr_history.h:139-142`): with `N > 10` several substeps share a bin, with `N < 10` some bins stay empty. The disk's gas sample is overwritten, so the last substep in a bin wins (`:169-170`), while the burst's accumulates (`:198-199`); the averages divide by `N` and `min(N, 10)` (`:232-240`). The metallicities `SfrDiskZ` and `SfrBulgeZ` are therefore means of point samples whose nodes are the substeps, and a burst's mass is recorded as `stars/dt` of one substep (`shared/sage_starburst_physics.h:451-452`), a rate that grows without bound as the step shrinks. Mimic already varies `N` per halo with dynamic substeps (`src/core/halo_evolution.c:179`). An adaptive integrator has no substeps, so these four outputs must be redefined against fixed sub-intervals of the snapshot interval. That is a change of definition, not of accuracy, and no bit comparison of them can survive it.

### 4. FIRE's redshift is a sampled-and-held forcing, and the package uses two

The FIRE loading reads `z = ZZ[SnapNum]` (`shared/sage_fire_feedback_physics.h:129`) for the disk path (`sage26_calculate_supernova_feedback.c:180`), bursts (`shared/sage_starburst_physics.h:378`) and FFB rows (`sage26_ffb_star_formation.c:460`), and the cold streams do the same (`shared/sage_cooling_physics.h:262`). The stored `SnapNum` is the snapshot a row was last written at: `current_snap − 1` for a newly created halo's row (`src/core/inheritance.c`, `init_new_halo`), and for an inherited row its progenitor's, which across a gapped link precedes `current_snap − 1`. So `z` is a held forcing from the row's previous snapshot, constant across the interval. The FFB classifier and the merger clock instead use the current snapshot's redshift (`sage26_determine_ffb_regime.c:241`; `sage26_initialise_merger_clock.c:179`). A continuous integrator that evaluates `z(t)` would change every FIRE loading and stream fraction: a science change to declare, not a refinement. The behaviour-preserving form is forcing held over the interval, which is Open Question 6's "terminate and restart at snapshot boundaries" applied to a quantity the brief did not list.

### 5. Rates whose time constant is the snapshot interval (shared with sage16, not recorded there)

Stripping removes `excess/N` each substep (`sage26_satellite_stripping.c:119`; `models/sage16/modules/sage_satellite_stripping/sage_satellite_stripping.c:82-83`): `d(excess)/dt = −excess/Δt_snap`, which removes `1 − 1/e` of the excess over an interval in the limit, not all of it (`sage26_satellite_stripping.c:111-118`). The rate has a limit, so it is correctly a rate, but its time constant is the tree's snapshot spacing rather than a physical timescale. A coupled package reproduces it only by declaring `Δt_snap` as a parameter of the law. The merger gate, conversely, already interpolates `Mvir` linearly inside the interval (`sage26_resolve_mergers_and_disruption.c:377-378`; `models/sage16/modules/sage_resolve_mergers_and_disruption/sage_resolve_mergers_and_disruption.c:186`): the one place where both packages already treat tree forcing as continuous within an interval. Open Question 6 should start from that precedent.

### 6. One jump cascade exists, and it terminates structurally

A merger-driven burst re-checks the disk and, if it is unstable, fires black-hole growth, a quasar wind and a second burst within the same event (`shared/sage_starburst_physics.h:528-564`). The re-check runs only for a merger burst (mode 0) below the major-merger threshold, and the second burst runs in mode 1 (`:528-529`, `:561-562`), so the cascade has depth two by construction. Open Question 5 needs a termination rule in general; for this package the rule is already in the code and can be stated as a bound.

### 7. Stored constitutive relations are re-clamped after every cold-gas debit

`H2gas` and `H1gas` are algebraic in `ColdGas`, the disk stars and `DiskScaleRadius` (#16), but they are stored, then capped at `X_H·ColdGas` after star formation (`shared/sage_sf_feedback_physics.h:180-190`), feedback (`:284-295`), black-hole growth (`shared/sage_agn_physics.h:298-309`) and bursts (`shared/sage_starburst_physics.h:498-512`). The caps exist only because an algebraic quantity goes stale when a later operator changes its argument. Evaluated inside `f`, they vanish; the output `H2gas` then becomes the value at commit rather than at the moment of star formation, another definitional change for the acceptance list.

### 8. The package already holds both forms, and one `Rheat` serves two channels

Two new modules compute and apply in one call: regime-aware cooling (#14) and the FFB transaction (#15). At R6 they replace the calculate/radio/apply chain (#11–#13) and, for FFB rows, the calculate/feedback/apply/enrich chain (#16–#18, #22). The split form is therefore not intrinsic to the physics. Inside #14, though, a `Regime` 1 galaxy runs the hot channel and then the CGM channel in sequence (`sage26_regime_aware_cooling.c:204-232`). Each reads and ratchets the one stored `Rheat` (`shared/sage_cooling_physics.h:846-850`, `:935-939`) against a different `rcool` (computed for the hot gas at `:454`; `Rvir` for the CGM at `:601`), so the CGM channel sees the ratchet the hot channel has just applied. This is an order dependence inside a single module, which permuting the run file cannot reveal and gate 3 would not test.

## What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled.** Open Question 1 is answered for every shipped `sage26` prescription, with evidence. At R6 the rate-shaped subset a coupled successor would have to reproduce (brief gate 2) is #8, #9, #10, #14, #15, #16, #17, #18 and #22, nine prescriptions; #11–#13 join it only with `CGMrecipeOn 0`. The jumps are the same four as in `sage16`, plus the regime-flip transfer inside #6. The new physics (CGM cooling, cold streams, H2 star formation, FIRE, FFB) is rate-shaped throughout and source-limited; none of it adds a jump.

**Not settled, and not settleable here.**
